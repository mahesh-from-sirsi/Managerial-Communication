Meaning, definition and nature of communication Communication is the process of sharing information, ideas, opinions, feelings or instructions between people through words, symbols, behaviour or other channels, with the aim of creating a common understanding and achieving a purpose. In management, communication is not complete merely because a sender has delivered a message. The receiver must interpret it sufficiently accurately for the intended action or understanding to occur. 1.1 Key features / nature ●​ Two-way and interactive: effective communication involves a response or feedback, not just one-way delivery. ●​ Purposeful: messages are sent to inform, persuade, instruct, coordinate, motivate or solve a problem. ●​ Continuous: managers communicate before, during and after decisions and activities. ●​ Dynamic and contextual: meaning can change with the situation, relationship, culture and channel. ●​ Verbal and non-verbal: words, tone, facial expressions, posture and gestures may all influence meaning. ●​ Symbolic: people use language, signs, numbers, images and gestures to represent ideas. ●​ Dependent on shared understanding: the sender and receiver need sufficiently common meanings. 1.2 Simple example A manager emails employees: “Submit the report soon.” The message has been sent, but “soon” is unclear. One employee submits it that evening; another assumes the deadline is Friday. A better message is: “Please upload the sales report to the shared folder by 4.00 p.m. on Thursday, 12 November.” The second message reduces ambiguity. Exam distinction Information transmission means sending data or a message. Communication is broader: it includes interpretation, shared meaning and feedback. 2. Communication in crisis management A crisis is an unexpected or serious situation that threatens people, operations, reputation, assets or the continuity of an organization. Crisis communication is the timely, accurate and coordinated sharing of information with affected stakeholders during such a situation. 9.1 Steps in crisis communication 1.​ Verify facts: establish what has happened, what is affected and what is still unknown. 2.​ Identify stakeholders: employees, customers, suppliers, regulators, media and the public, as relevant. 3.​ Coordinate responsibility: identify the authorized spokesperson or communication owner. 4.​ Communicate promptly: do not allow avoidable silence to create confusion or rumours. 5.​ State known and unknown information: do not speculate or make unsupported promises. 6.​ Give clear instructions: explain what stakeholders should do, avoid or expect. 7.​ Provide regular updates: state when and where the next update will be shared. 8.​ Review and recover: correct inaccurate information, explain recovery steps and learn from the incident. 9.2 Principles of effective crisis communication Principle Why it matters A prompt response matters, but unverified Speed with accuracy information can worsen the crisis.
