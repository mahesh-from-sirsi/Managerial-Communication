Supplement: Business presentations and advanced visual aids

Plan a presentation around one audience, one purpose and a clear takeaway. A useful structure is opening/context → key message → evidence/analysis → recommendation → close and call to action. Visual aids may include charts, tables, process diagrams, timelines, dashboards, photographs and short video. Choose the visual to match the information: bars for comparisons, lines for trends, tables for exact values, process diagrams for steps. Keep slides readable, label axes, cite data, use consistent design and never overload slides with paragraphs.

Practice: Present a three-slide recommendation to improve student placement readiness: problem, evidence, recommended action.

Supplement: Negotiation process

  1. Prepare: clarify objectives, interests, facts, limits and alternatives.
  2. Open: establish purpose, participants and ground rules.
  3. Explore: ask questions and understand each party’s interests.
  4. Bargain: develop options and evaluate trade-offs.
  5. Close: confirm the agreement, responsibilities and deadlines.
  6. Implement and review: follow up and resolve gaps.

Supplement: Negotiation strategies and influencing factors

Common approaches include collaborative/problem-solving (seek mutual gains), competitive/distributive (claim value where interests conflict), accommodating (prioritise relationship or the other party’s need), avoiding (delay or disengage when appropriate) and compromising (each side gives up something). Strategy depends on importance of outcome, relationship, time pressure, power, information, alternatives, culture, trust and ethical constraints. Avoid false claims, coercion and promises that cannot be honoured.

10. Integrated Case Studies Case 1 — The 12% Project Delay Case: A college ERP implementation is 12% behind schedule. The project manager has data showing delays in vendor configuration and user acceptance testing. The principal wants a decision on whether to add temporary resources or extend the deadline. Suggested answer logic: ●​ Use a short management report. ●​ Include purpose, scope, evidence/findings, analysis, conclusion and recommendation. ●​ Do not recommend from the delay percentage alone; identify causes, remaining work, cost, risk and alternative recovery options. ●​ Recommendation should be specific, evidence-based and implementable. Case 2 — Proposal for a Student Analytics Pilot Case: An MBA department wants approval for a 12-week analytics programme for 60 students using existing infrastructure. The sponsor asks for a proposal. Suggested answer logic: ●​ Problem → objective → solution → resources/budget → schedule → benefits → risks → evaluation → approval request. ●​ Use measurable outcomes, not vague claims such as 'students will become excellent'. ●​ Risks might include attendance, faculty capacity and uneven student skill levels. Case 3 — Product Recall and Media Pressure Case: A consumer product has a confirmed quality issue. Journalists ask whether the organization knew about the problem earlier. The facts are still being verified. Suggested answer logic: ●​ State what is confirmed and what is still under verification. ●​ Do not invent a date, cause or number. ●​ A press conference may be appropriate when the issue is significant and a coordinated public response is needed; the decision depends on the circumstances. ●​ Avoid speculation, blaming individuals without evidence and misleading reassurance. Case 4 — Meeting That Produces No Decision Case: A 14-person committee meets for two hours. There is no agenda. Three people dominate the discussion.
