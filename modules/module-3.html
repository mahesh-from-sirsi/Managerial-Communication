Supplement: Professional CV and application package

A CV should be truthful, relevant, readable and tailored to the role. Typical sections: contact details, professional summary/objective, education, skills, projects/internships, experience (if any), certifications, achievements and references when requested. Use action verbs and measurable evidence; do not invent experience or skills. A covering message should identify the role, state fit briefly and attach the CV using a professional file name.

Supplement: Group discussion skills

Prepare by understanding the topic and organising two or three points. During the discussion: enter politely, make a relevant point, support it with an example, listen, build on others’ ideas, disagree respectfully, invite quieter members and help summarise. Avoid interrupting, dominating, repeating points or making unsupported claims.

Practice: “Should organisations allow employees to use generative AI for routine work?” Discuss for six minutes. Observe content, clarity, listening, teamwork and respectful disagreement.

Supplement: Interview skills

Before: research the organisation and role, review the CV, prepare examples and plan logistics. During: listen to the full question, answer directly, use a situation–action–result structure for experience questions, ask relevant questions and remain professional. After: send a concise thank-you note and follow up appropriately.

Practice question: “Tell me about a time you handled a disagreement in a team.” Students can prepare using Situation, Task, Action and Result.

Supplement: Technology-enabled communication

Email supports formal asynchronous messages and records; intranet supports internal information sharing; video conferencing supports remote meetings and presentations; collaboration platforms support shared documents, tasks and team coordination. Select tools based on urgency, sensitivity, audience access, record requirements and confidentiality. Apply digital etiquette: clear subject lines, concise messages, appropriate recipients, secure links, mute discipline, camera awareness and follow-up records.

Integrated Case Studies Case 1: Delayed Product Launch A company planned to launch a student-finance application on 1 December. Testing has uncovered a critical security issue. The launch must move to 20 December. Marketing has already announced 1 December publicly. Issues for analysis: The internal message to engineering and operations should confirm the revised plan; the message to marketing should explain the delay; the request to the CEO should justify additional testing resources; and the thank-you message should recognize the security team's contribution. Suggested analysis: Each message has a different audience and purpose. The revised plan is a neutral or positive internal update; the delay is bad news for marketing; the request for resources is persuasive; and the appreciation note is a thank-you message. Audience analysis and message strategy should therefore differ. Case 2: The Customer Refusal A customer requests a refund 45 days after purchase. Company policy permits refunds within 30 days, but the product has a confirmed manufacturing defect. The customer is angry. Suggested analysis: The confirmed defect is an important fact and should not be ignored. A policy-only refusal may damage goodwill and may fail to address the defect. The writer should distinguish the normal refund period from the warranty or quality process, explain the actual decision and offer a fair remedy if permitted by company policy.
