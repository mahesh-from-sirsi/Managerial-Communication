## Learning outcomes
Explain communication models and classifications; choose appropriate channels; prepare accurate workplace messages; manage disagreement and uncertainty; adapt cross-culturally; diagnose barriers and remedies.

## Unit 1
<a id="unit-1"></a>
### Meaning, role and purpose
**Understand:** Define managerial communication; distinguish informing, persuading, coordinating, directing and relationship-building.

**Workplace example:** A supervisor must explain why a delivery schedule changed.

**Apply:** Write a 100-word employee update stating purpose, audience and action.

**Self-check:** What is the intended outcome? Who needs the message? Which facts are confirmed? What response or feedback proves understanding?

## Unit 2

### Classification and channels
**Understand:** Compare oral/written, verbal/non-verbal, formal/informal, synchronous/asynchronous and upward/downward/horizontal/diagonal communication.

**Workplace example:** A suspected data leak requires an immediate escalation and a written incident record.

**Apply:** Choose channels for five situations; justify urgency, privacy and auditability.

**Self-check:** What is the intended outcome? Who needs the message? Which facts are confirmed? What response or feedback proves understanding?

## Unit 3
<a id="unit-3"></a>
### Communication process
**Understand:** Explain sender, encoding, message, channel, decoding, receiver, feedback, noise and context.

**Workplace example:** 'Send latest figures quickly' produces incomparable branch reports.

**Apply:** Rewrite instruction with metric, period, format, recipient and deadline; propose feedback check.

**Self-check:** What is the intended outcome? Who needs the message? Which facts are confirmed? What response or feedback proves understanding?

## Unit 4
<a id="unit-4"></a>
### Effective communication
**Understand:** Apply clarity, completeness, conciseness, correctness, consideration, courtesy and concreteness; avoid treating the 7Cs as a substitute for factual accuracy.

**Workplace example:** An HR reminder blames employees without identifying missing documents.

**Apply:** Rewrite an email with precise action, deadline and support contact.

**Self-check:** What is the intended outcome? Who needs the message? Which facts are confirmed? What response or feedback proves understanding?

## Unit 6
<a id="unit-6"></a>
### Conflict resolution
**Understand:** Distinguish positions from interests; use active listening, neutral framing, options and documented agreements.

**Workplace example:** Sales and Operations blame each other for 200 hampers due tomorrow.

**Apply:** Write neutral incident summary, feasible options and customer update.

**Self-check:** What is the intended outcome? Who needs the message? Which facts are confirmed? What response or feedback proves understanding?

## Unit 7
<a id="unit-7"></a>
### Crisis management and negotiation
**Understand:** Use verified facts, unknowns, owners and update times in crises; prepare BATNA, interests, evidence and alternatives in negotiations.

**Workplace example:** Tracking system shows false deliveries; a candidate negotiates ₹4.2L offer.

**Apply:** Draft a holding statement and evidence-based negotiation email.

**Self-check:** What is the intended outcome? Who needs the message? Which facts are confirmed? What response or feedback proves understanding?

## Unit 8
<a id="unit-8"></a>
### Cross-cultural communication
**Understand:** Discuss directness, hierarchy, time zones, idioms, assumptions and confirmation of commitments without stereotyping.

**Workplace example:** A colleague says 'we will try' and overseas client interprets it as a promise.

**Apply:** Write a clarification that confirms scope, date and decision owner.

**Self-check:** What is the intended outcome? Who needs the message? Which facts are confirmed? What response or feedback proves understanding?

## Unit 9
<a id="unit-9"></a>
### Barriers and remedies
**Understand:** Identify semantic, emotional, physical, technological, cultural, organisational and information-overload barriers.

**Workplace example:** A noisy shift briefing is misunderstood despite staff nodding.

**Apply:** Design teach-back and feedback checks; evaluate which barriers remain.

**Self-check:** What is the intended outcome? Who needs the message? Which facts are confirmed? What response or feedback proves understanding?
