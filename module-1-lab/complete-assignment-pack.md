# Module 1 — Complete Student Assignment Pack

**VTU MBA Managerial Communication | Fictional workplace cases | Version 1**

## How to submit
Complete each assigned task independently unless faculty explicitly assigns a group. Submit a PDF or Markdown file named `A##_StudentID` with: (1) case title and audience, (2) original response, (3) revised response, (4) 100–150-word reasoning explaining channel, tone and risks, (5) 50–100-word reflection, (6) AI assistance disclosure and source references if applicable. Do not publish real personal, company or customer information. The sample messages below are illustrative, not full model answers.

**Marking rubric /100:** message clarity 20; language 15; professional tone 15; completeness 20; situational judgement 20; structure/conciseness 10. Faculty should give formative feedback on the first draft and assess the revised submission. For spoken tasks, assess clarity, pace, listening and responses separately.

**Suggested schedule:** A01–A04 weeks 1–2; A05–A08 weeks 3–4; A09–A12 weeks 5–6. Faculty may adjust deadlines. The tasks below are fictional simulations.

## A01 — Email Repair

### Workplace brief
Ramya, HR executive, must collect pending payroll documents. Her draft reads: 'You people are not sending documents properly. I told many times. Do the needful ASAP.'

### Student instructions
Write a subject line and 120–160-word email specifying action, fictional deadline and support channel. Annotate five problems in original.

### Example response or opening
> Subject: Action required: pending payroll documents by 24 November. Dear Team, Please submit outstanding payroll documents by 5 PM on 24 November. If already submitted, disregard this reminder. Contact HR for assistance. Regards, Ramya.

### Quality and ethical checks
Avoid threatening salary consequences without confirmed policy. Evaluate whether the request is specific and courteous.

### Deliverables
- Draft communication(s) requested above, with recipient and appropriate subject/title.
- A brief rationale identifying the communication objective, audience, channel and possible misunderstanding.
- Revised version highlighting at least two improvements after self- or peer-review.
- Reflection: What would change if the recipient challenged your message?

### Assessment focus
Apply the common 100-point rubric; award situational-judgement credit for verified facts, appropriate escalation and realistic commitments.

## Student self-review checklist
- [ ] The audience and purpose are explicit.
- [ ] Facts, assumptions and unknowns are distinguished.
- [ ] The required action, owner and time are clear where relevant.
- [ ] Tone is respectful, including in disagreement.
- [ ] No fabricated policies, promises or confidential information appear.
- [ ] The final version improves measurably on the first draft.

## Faculty use
These examples are visible to students and should be treated as scaffolding. For graded work, vary fictional dates, figures or constraints, and keep detailed marking keys privately. Existing course notes and quizzes remain accessible through the main repository.
