Case Study Bank

Short, classroom-ready cases mapped to the five modules. These are instructional scenarios, not historical events.

Suggested method: 2 minutes to read, 4 minutes to discuss in pairs/groups, 2 minutes to present one recommendation.
MODULE 1

The delayed instruction

A regional sales manager messages branch teams: “Send the latest numbers quickly.” One branch sends monthly revenue, another sends weekly orders, and a third waits for a format. The review meeting begins without comparable data.

Discuss / Present

Identify the ambiguity, map the communication process, name barriers, rewrite the instruction and propose a feedback check.

Concept mapping: Communication process; clarity; barriers; organisational channels.

MODULE 1

The product launch at risk

Engineering discovers a defect shortly before launch. Marketing has announced the date, sales has promised availability and customer support has no approved response.

Discuss / Present

List stakeholders; separate verified facts from unknowns; draft an internal update; propose a crisis communication sequence and negotiation plan.

Concept mapping: Crisis communication; conflict resolution; negotiation; cross-functional communication.

MODULE 2

The interviewer who does not listen

During a placement interview, the interviewer interrupts candidates, checks a phone and asks unrelated questions. Candidates give short answers and fail to explain their experience.

Discuss / Present

Identify listening barriers and non-verbal signals; redesign the interviewer's listening behaviour; suggest open questions.

Concept mapping: Listening process; empathy; conversation control; non-verbal communication.

MODULE 2

The misunderstood instruction

A supervisor explains a new process quickly in a noisy workshop. Workers nod, but later follow different steps.

Discuss / Present

Identify oral communication barriers; propose a better mode, pace, demonstration and teach-back check.

Concept mapping: Oral communication; listening; feedback; non-verbal cues.

MODULE 3

The customer refusal

A supplier cannot meet a customer's requested delivery date. The sales executive must refuse the date while preserving the relationship and offering alternatives.

Discuss / Present

Choose a negative-message strategy; draft the message; use reader-oriented language and a constructive close.

Concept mapping: Audience analysis; negative messages; tone; business writing.

MODULE 3

The CV that overclaims

A candidate's CV lists advanced analytics and leadership skills but gives no evidence. In the interview, the candidate cannot explain the claims.

Discuss / Present

Identify credibility issues; rewrite two CV bullets using truthful evidence; prepare a concise interview response.

Concept mapping: CV writing; employment communication; accuracy; ethics.

MODULE 4

The report with no recommendation

A department report contains many pages of data but no analysis, conclusion or requested decision. Senior management cannot determine what action is needed.

Discuss / Present

Identify report weaknesses; create an executive summary outline; state evidence, alternatives and recommendation.

Concept mapping: Business reports; proposals; audience focus; presentation.

MODULE 4

The meeting that produced no decision

A 14-person committee meets for two hours without a clear agenda. Three members dominate; no decision owner is present; the meeting ends with “we will discuss again”.

Discuss / Present

Redesign purpose, participants, agenda and time; draft action-oriented minutes with owners and deadlines.

Concept mapping: Meetings; group communication; agenda; minutes.

MODULE 5

The student services app decision

A college team must decide whether to launch a student services app before the next semester. User feedback is mixed, the budget is limited and accessibility testing is incomplete.

Discuss / Present

Define the central issue; distinguish facts and assumptions; develop three options; compare risks and recommend an implementation plan.

Concept mapping: Case method; analysis; alternatives; recommendation.

MODULE 5

The misleading placement claim

A training provider advertises “100% placement guaranteed” although it has no employer commitments. A student shares personal details and pays a fee based on the claim.

Discuss / Present

Identify possible misrepresentation and privacy concerns; list verification steps; propose ethical corrective communication.

Concept mapping: Etiquette; privacy; misrepresentation; fraud awareness.
