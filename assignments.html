MODULE 3 · Written & Employment Communication

A01 — Email Repair

Learning outcome: Diagnose communication failures; plan a channel strategy; draft and revise a professional payroll reminder.

Workplace brief

Ramya is an HR executive at a fictional Bengaluru manufacturer with 250 employees. Payroll processing closes tomorrow at 4 PM. Twelve employees appear to have outstanding documents. Eight work in production and rarely check email during shifts; four work in administration. Two employees say they already submitted documents, but HR has not verified the records. HR has no authority to threaten salary deductions.

Unsent draft — Subject: URGENT!!!

You people are not sending documents properly. I told many times. Nobody is taking HR seriously. Do the needful ASAP. If salary is delayed, don't blame HR.

Ramya

Student tasks

  1. Diagnose (7 minutes): Identify six specific communication failures, classify each (clarity, tone, channel, feedback, assumption or accuracy) and explain the consequence.
  2. Plan (5 minutes): Map sender, audience, message, channel, noise and feedback. Explain why a mass email alone may fail.
  3. Write (10 minutes): Draft an email of at most 160 words with an actionable subject, verified or conditional wording, feasible deadline, submission route and support contact. Recommend a separate way to reach production employees.
  4. Respond (5 minutes): An employee replies, “I submitted everything last week. Why are you accusing me?” Write a respectful reply of at most 70 words and explain how HR will verify the record.
  5. Revise (8 minutes): Peer-review, make two meaningful changes and justify them.

Required submission and assessment

Submit a six-row diagnosis table, communication-process map, channel decision, first and final email, employee-response draft and two-change review log. Use fictional contact details; never invent company policy.

Assessment /100: diagnosis 20; communication process 15; channel judgement 15; email clarity and professionalism 20; conflict response 15; revision and reasoning 15.

Instructor twist: Six production employees were never told which documents were required. Ask students to revise their attribution of responsibility and communication plan.

Module alignment: Primary Module 3 (effective business writing); reinforces Module 1 (communication process and barriers). One assignment does not cover either entire module.

Professional standard: Distinguish facts from assumptions, protect confidentiality, use respectful language and avoid unsupported commitments.

MODULE 2 · Oral, Listening & Non-verbal Communication

A02 — The 60-Second Management Update

Learning outcome: Practise concise oral briefing, active listening and confirmation.

Workplace brief

A customer onboarding project is 3 days late. Data validation is incomplete; the team can finish by Friday if Finance confirms the revised format by Wednesday.

Student tasks

Deliver the 60-second update aloud without reading word-for-word. A partner plays the manager and asks two clarifying questions. Repeat the update after reflecting back the questions; identify which information is confirmed and which is conditional.

Required submission and assessment

Submit a five-line status note, speaking outline, two questions received, answers and a brief reflection on pace, clarity and listening. Assess oral delivery, audience awareness and feedback—not only the written script.

Professional standard: Distinguish facts from assumptions, protect confidentiality, use respectful language and avoid unsupported commitments.

MODULE 1 · Foundations of Managerial Communication

A03 — Choose the Communication Channel

Learning outcome: Choose and defend appropriate channels for different organisational messages.

Workplace brief

Decide how to handle (a) suspected data breach, (b) holiday list, (c) supervisor conflict, (d) revised delivery promise, (e) new employee needing instructions.

Student tasks

For all five scenarios, compare at least two channels and explain urgency, sensitivity, audience access, required acknowledgement and documentary trail. Rank the consequences of choosing the wrong channel.

Required submission and assessment

Submit a five-row decision matrix plus one revised choice after a peer challenges your assumption. For a suspected breach, use the organisation's approved incident process rather than a public group chat.

Professional standard: Distinguish facts from assumptions, protect confidentiality, use respectful language and avoid unsupported commitments.

MODULE 1 · Foundations of Managerial Communication

A04 — The Missing Instructions

Learning outcome: Identify ambiguity, feedback failures and corrective communication.

Workplace brief

A regional manager messages three branches: 'Send latest sales numbers quickly.' One sends revenue, another orders, and the third waits.

Student tasks

List six ambiguities and explain the different branch interpretations. Draft an instruction that specifies the metric, reporting period, format, recipient, owner and deadline. Design a teach-back acknowledgement that confirms understanding, not merely receipt.

Required submission and assessment

Submit an ambiguity table, corrected instruction and example confirmation from a branch. Explain how feedback would detect a remaining misunderstanding.

Professional standard: Distinguish facts from assumptions, protect confidentiality, use respectful language and avoid unsupported commitments.

MODULE 3 · Written & Employment Communication

A05 — The Difficult No

Learning outcome: Compose an appropriately cautious negative workplace message.

Workplace brief

An employee requests five days of leave during a critical audit. The manager has not confirmed staffing alternatives or applicable leave policy.

Student tasks

Separate facts, unknown leave-policy requirements and possible alternatives. Draft a respectful reply that does not prejudge approval. Prepare a short response to: “I have already booked travel; why are you rejecting me?”

Required submission and assessment

Submit the initial reply, policy-verification questions, employee follow-up response and a revision showing how the message balances empathy and operational needs. Reinforces Module 1 conflict management.

Professional standard: Distinguish facts from assumptions, protect confidentiality, use respectful language and avoid unsupported commitments.

MODULE 1 · Foundations of Managerial Communication

A07 — The Blame Game

Learning outcome: Resolve interdepartmental conflict through fact-based communication.

Workplace brief

Sales promised 200 gift hampers by tomorrow without checking Operations. Operations can currently confirm only 120. Customer paid advance and needs all 200 for an event.

Student tasks

Distinguish accountability from blame. Build a three-option recovery matrix with feasibility and unknowns. Draft internal and customer communications. After the customer rejects partial delivery, revise the plan without inventing stock availability or compensation authority.

Required submission and assessment

Submit incident facts/unknowns table, option matrix, two messages and revised decision. Assess conflict handling, customer trust and realistic commitments.

Professional standard: Distinguish facts from assumptions, protect confidentiality, use respectful language and avoid unsupported commitments.

MODULE 4 · Advanced Business Communication

A08 — Salary Negotiation

Learning outcome: Practise evidence-led negotiation and spoken persuasion.

Workplace brief

Hareesh is offered ₹4.2 lakh annual CTC, expects ₹5 lakh, and has relevant project evidence. HR indicates a standard ₹4–4.8 lakh range, not guaranteed flexibility.

Student tasks

Define Hareesh's target, acceptable alternatives and walk-away considerations without assuming HR flexibility. Prepare a negotiation email and a 60-second spoken pitch. Pair with a student acting as HR, who must challenge the requested amount. Reflect on the counteroffer.

Required submission and assessment

Submit preparation matrix, email, speaking script, fallback choices, counteroffer response and professional acceptance or rejection draft. Reinforces Module 1 negotiation concepts.

Professional standard: Distinguish facts from assumptions, protect confidentiality, use respectful language and avoid unsupported commitments.

A09 — Crisis at 9 AM

Learning outcome: Communicate responsibly as incident facts evolve.

Workplace brief

Fictional grocery app receives 80 delivery complaints. Tracking incorrectly displays deliveries. At 9:00 cause unknown; 9:20 technical malfunction confirmed but individual orders unverified.

Student tasks

Create a 9:00 internal alert using only confirmed facts. At 9:20 update it after the technical malfunction is confirmed. Draft a public holding statement (60 words maximum) and a 9:30 leadership briefing. State what remains unknown and when the next update will occur.

Required submission and assessment

Submit a timestamped fact log, two internal updates, public statement and management brief. Assess accuracy, sequencing, audience selection and avoidance of unsupported promises.

Professional standard: Distinguish facts from assumptions, protect confidentiality, use respectful language and avoid unsupported commitments.

MODULE 5 · Case Analysis, Etiquette & Legal Aspects

A11 — The Rumour Problem

Learning outcome: Apply managerial etiquette, confidentiality and responsible case judgement.

Workplace brief

An unverified message claims layoffs will be announced. Staff are anxious. HR has no authorised confirmation and cannot share confidential personnel decisions.

Student tasks

Distinguish what HR knows, may disclose and must verify. Compare a public reassurance, private escalation and silence, identifying the risks of each. Draft an authorised holding statement and a confidential request for leadership guidance.

Required submission and assessment

Submit a decision table, holding statement, confidential escalation and verification checklist. Discuss privacy, unsupported assurances and the limits of HR authority. Also reinforces Module 1 crisis communication.

MODULE 4 · Advanced Business Communication

A12 — The CEO Brief

Learning outcome: Write an executive decision brief supported by business facts.

Workplace brief

Fictional retail unit reports revenue ₹18 lakh against ₹20 lakh target, 14 delayed orders, 9 resolved complaints, and a supplier bottleneck. Management must choose an alternate supplier trial.

Student tasks

Calculate the revenue shortfall and percentage accurately. Distinguish the supplier bottleneck from unproven causes of the revenue gap. Present two viable management options, the decision required, owner and review date in a concise executive brief.

Required submission and assessment

Submit a 100-word executive brief, five-line decision log and one-minute oral explanation of your recommendation. Assess decision usefulness, accuracy and concision.

Professional standard: Distinguish facts from assumptions, protect confidentiality, use respectful language and avoid unsupported commitments.

Student self-review checklist

☐ The audience and purpose are explicit.

☐ Facts, assumptions and unknowns are distinguished.

☐ The required action, owner and time are clear where relevant.

☐ Tone is respectful, including in disagreement.

☐ No fabricated policies, promises or confidential information appear.

☐ The final version improves measurably on the first draft.

Faculty use

For graded work, vary fictional dates, figures or constraints, and keep detailed marking keys privately. Require students to defend decisions orally or respond to an unseen twist to assess understanding. Existing course notes and quizzes remain accessible through the main repository.
