A01 — Email Repair

Workplace brief

Ramya, HR executive, must collect pending payroll documents. Her draft reads: 'You people are not sending documents properly. I told many times. Do the needful ASAP.'

Student instructions

Write a subject line and 120–160-word email specifying action, fictional deadline and support channel. Annotate five problems in original.

Example response or opening

> Subject: Action required: pending payroll documents by 24 November. Dear Team, Please submit outstanding payroll documents by 5 PM on 24 November. If already submitted, disregard this reminder. Contact HR for assistance. Regards, Ramya.

Quality and ethical checks

Avoid threatening salary consequences without confirmed policy. Evaluate whether the request is specific and courteous.

Deliverables

• Draft communication(s) requested above, with recipient and appropriate subject/title.

• A brief rationale identifying the communication objective, audience, channel and possible misunderstanding.

• Revised version highlighting at least two improvements after self- or peer-review.

• Reflection: What would change if the recipient challenged your message?

Assessment focus

Apply the common 100-point rubric; award situational-judgement credit for verified facts, appropriate escalation and realistic commitments.

A02 — The 60-Second Management Update

Workplace brief

A customer onboarding project is 3 days late. Data validation is incomplete; the team can finish by Friday if Finance confirms the revised format by Wednesday.

A04 — The Missing Instructions

Workplace brief

A regional manager messages three branches: 'Send latest sales numbers quickly.' One sends revenue, another orders, and the third waits.

Student instructions

List six ambiguities; draft corrected instruction specifying metric, period, units, template, recipient and deadline; create acknowledgement message.

Example response or opening

A05 — The Difficult No

Workplace brief

An employee requests five days of leave during a critical audit. The manager has not confirmed staffing alternatives or applicable leave policy.

Student instructions

Draft a respectful 130–180-word reply, avoiding unsupported policy claims; request essential information and suggest feasible alternatives.

Example response or opening

> 'I understand the importance of your request. I cannot confirm approval yet because audit coverage needs review. Could we discuss dates and handover options tomorrow?'

Quality and ethical checks

Do not reveal other employees' personal details or promise a decision prematurely.

A07 — The Blame Game

Workplace brief

Sales promised 200 gift hampers by tomorrow without checking Operations. Operations can currently confirm only 120. Customer paid advance and needs all 200 for an event.

Student instructions

Write 150-word neutral internal incident summary, 3-option recovery matrix, and 120-word customer update. Twist: customer rejects partial delivery; revise.

Example response or opening

> 'We are reviewing options to fulfil the agreed quantity. We will confirm a feasible recovery plan by 4 PM today; we regret the uncertainty.'

Quality and ethical checks

Separate internal accountability from external message; do not guarantee unverified recovery.

A08 — Salary Negotiation

Workplace brief

Hareesh is offered ₹4.2 lakh annual CTC, expects ₹5 lakh, and has relevant project evidence. HR indicates a standard ₹4–4.8 lakh range, not guaranteed flexibility.

A11 — The Rumour Problem

Workplace brief

An unverified message claims layoffs will be announced. Staff are anxious. HR has no authorised confirmation and cannot share confidential personnel decisions.

A12 — The CEO Brief

Workplace brief

Fictional retail unit reports revenue ₹18 lakh against ₹20 lakh target, 14 delayed orders, 9 resolved complaints, and a supplier bottleneck. Management must choose an alternate supplier trial.

Student instructions

Write <=100-word executive brief and a 5-line decision log; include gap, customer impact, options, owner, decision required and review date.

Example response or opening

> 'Revenue was ₹18 lakh versus ₹20 lakh target (10% below). Fourteen orders were delayed; nine complaints were resolved. Approval requested for a controlled alternate-supplier trial.'

Quality and ethical checks

Numbers are fictional. Avoid attributing all revenue gap to supplier without evidence.

Deliverables

• Draft communication(s) requested above, with recipient and appropriate subject/title.

• A brief rationale identifying the communication objective, audience, channel and possible misunderstanding.

• Revised version highlighting at least two improvements after self- or peer-review.

Student self-review checklist

• [ ] The audience and purpose are explicit.

• [ ] Facts, assumptions and unknowns are distinguished.

• [ ] The required action, owner and time are clear where relevant.

• [ ] Tone is respectful, including in disagreement.

• [ ] No fabricated policies, promises or confidential information appear.

• [ ] The final version improves measurably on the first draft.
