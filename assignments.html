MODULE 3 · Written & Employment Communication

A01 — Email Repair

Learning outcome: Diagnose communication failures; plan a channel strategy; draft and revise a professional payroll reminder.

Workplace brief

Ramya is an HR executive at a fictional Bengaluru manufacturer with 250 employees. Payroll processing closes tomorrow at 4 PM. Twelve employees appear to have outstanding documents. Eight work in production and rarely check email during shifts; four work in administration. Two employees say they already submitted documents, but HR has not verified the records. HR has no authority to threaten salary deductions.

Unsent draft — Subject: URGENT!!!

You people are not sending documents properly. I told many times. Nobody is taking HR seriously. Do the needful ASAP. If salary is delayed, don't blame HR.

Ramya

Student tasks

  1. Diagnose (7 minutes): Identify six specific communication failures, classify each (clarity, tone, channel, feedback, assumption or accuracy) and explain the consequence.
  2. Plan (5 minutes): Map sender, audience, message, channel, noise and feedback. Explain why a mass email alone may fail.
  3. Write (10 minutes): Draft an email of at most 160 words with an actionable subject, verified or conditional wording, feasible deadline, submission route and support contact. Recommend a separate way to reach production employees.
  4. Respond (5 minutes): An employee replies, “I submitted everything last week. Why are you accusing me?” Write a respectful reply of at most 70 words and explain how HR will verify the record.
  5. Revise (8 minutes): Peer-review, make two meaningful changes and justify them.

Required submission and assessment

Submit a six-row diagnosis table, communication-process map, channel decision, first and final email, employee-response draft and two-change review log. Use fictional contact details; never invent company policy.

Assessment /100: diagnosis 20; communication process 15; channel judgement 15; email clarity and professionalism 20; conflict response 15; revision and reasoning 15.

Instructor twist: Six production employees were never told which documents were required. Ask students to revise their attribution of responsibility and communication plan.

Module alignment: Primary Module 3 (effective business writing); reinforces Module 1 (communication process and barriers). One assignment does not cover either entire module.

Professional standard: Distinguish facts from assumptions, protect confidentiality, use respectful language and avoid unsupported commitments.

A02 — The 60-Second Management Update

Learning outcome: Practise concise oral briefing, active listening and confirmation.

Workplace brief

A customer onboarding project is 3 days late. Data validation is incomplete; the team can finish by Friday if Finance confirms the revised format by Wednesday.

Student tasks

Deliver the 60-second update aloud without reading word-for-word. A partner plays the manager and asks two clarifying questions. Repeat the update after reflecting back the questions; identify which information is confirmed and which is conditional.

Required submission and assessment

Submit a five-line status note, speaking outline, two questions received, answers and a brief reflection on pace, clarity and listening. Assess oral delivery, audience awareness and feedback—not only the written script.

Professional standard: Distinguish facts from assumptions, protect confidentiality, use respectful language and avoid unsupported commitments.

MODULE 1 · Foundations of Managerial Communication

A03 — Choose the Communication Channel

Learning outcome: Choose and defend appropriate channels for different organisational messages.

Workplace brief

Decide how to handle (a) suspected data breach, (b) holiday list, (c) supervisor conflict, (d) revised delivery promise, (e) new employee needing instructions.

Student tasks

For all five scenarios, compare at least two channels and explain urgency, sensitivity, audience access, required acknowledgement and documentary trail. Rank the consequences of choosing the wrong channel.

Required submission and assessment

Submit a five-row decision matrix plus one revised choice after a peer challenges your assumption. For a suspected breach, use the organisation's approved incident process rather than a public group chat.

Student self-review checklist

  • Audience and purpose are explicit.
  • Facts, assumptions and unknowns are distinguished.
  • Required action, owner and time are clear where relevant.
  • Tone remains respectful, including in disagreement.
  • No fabricated policies, promises or confidential information appear.
  • The final version measurably improves on the first draft.

Faculty use

For graded work, vary fictional dates, figures or constraints and keep detailed marking keys privately. Require students to defend decisions orally or respond to an unseen twist to assess understanding. Existing course notes and quizzes remain accessible through the main repository.
